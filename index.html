How It Works

TMS Stimulates the Brain Directly — Without Medication

Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation uses a focused magnetic field to deliver electrical pulses to the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex — the region consistently underactive in treatment-resistant depression. No drugs enter your system. No anesthesia. You sit in a chair for 20–40 minutes, remain fully awake, and drive home afterward.

🧠

Different Mechanism — Not Another Antidepressant

SSRIs and SNRIs adjust neurotransmitter levels chemically. TMS directly stimulates neural circuits using magnetic fields — the same physics as an MRI. That's why it works when medications haven't.

✅

FDA-Cleared, Insurance-Covered

TMS has been FDA-cleared for treatment-resistant depression since 2008 and for OCD since 2018. Most major insurers — BCBS, Cigna, Aetna, UnitedHealthcare — cover it after documented failure of 2+ antidepressants.

🚗

Fully Outpatient — No Downtime

Sessions run 20–40 minutes. You are awake and alert throughout. No sedation, no recovery time. Most patients schedule sessions before or after work and drive themselves home.

📊

~58% Response Rate in Resistant Cases

The O'Reardon et al. multisite trial and subsequent real-world data show approximately 58% of treatment-resistant patients respond to TMS — with ~37% achieving full remission. That's a meaningful clinical result when medications have repeatedly failed.

What TMS Treats in Charlotte

TMS has FDA clearance for four indications. Here's what the evidence shows for each.

Treatment-Resistant Depression

The primary indication. FDA-cleared 2008. Covered by most insurance after 2+ antidepressant failures. ~58% response, ~37% remission in published trials.

OCD

FDA-cleared 2018 (deep TMS via Brainsway H7 coil). For patients with OCD inadequately controlled by medications and behavioral therapy.

Anxious Depression

FDA-cleared 2021. Specifically indicated for major depression with comorbid anxiety symptoms — a common presentation that responds poorly to standard antidepressants alone.

Smoking Cessation

FDA-cleared 2020. Deep TMS targeting the insula and prefrontal cortex has shown efficacy in reducing cravings for patients who have not quit with standard methods.

What to Expect

A Standard TMS Course

TMS is not a one-session treatment. Here is exactly what a standard course looks like.

1

Physician Consultation

A board-certified psychiatrist reviews your diagnosis, medication history, and any contraindications (metal implants near the head, seizure history). This determines whether you qualify and which TMS protocol fits your case.

2

Insurance Verification

Our team checks your coverage before you commit. Most major plans — BCBS NC, Cigna, Aetna, UHC — cover TMS after 2 antidepressant failures. We confirm your specific benefits and out-of-pocket estimate upfront.

3

Motor Threshold Calibration

Your first treatment session calibrates the magnetic pulse intensity to your individual threshold — ensuring precise targeting of the DLPFC at the correct dose. This takes slightly longer than subsequent sessions.

4

36 Sessions Over 6–8 Weeks

Standard protocol: 5 sessions per week for 6–8 weeks. Each session is 20–40 minutes. You sit in a reclined chair, fully awake. Most patients begin noticing improvement after weeks 3–4.

5

Response Assessment

At the end of the course, your psychiatrist assesses your response using standardized scales (PHQ-9, HAM-D). If you've responded well, a maintenance plan is discussed. If not, alternatives are reviewed honestly.

Insurance Coverage

TMS Is Covered — Unlike Most Other Alternatives

This is TMS's biggest practical advantage over ketamine infusions, which are almost never covered. Here's what coverage typically looks like.

📋

Standard Criteria

Most insurers require a DSM-5 diagnosis of major depressive disorder and documented failure of at least 2 antidepressants at adequate doses. Some require a prior authorization letter from your prescribing physician.

🏥

Insurers That Cover TMS

BCBS North Carolina, Cigna, Aetna, UnitedHealthcare, and most other major commercial plans. Medicare covers TMS for treatment-resistant depression. Medicaid coverage varies by state.

💰

Out-of-Pocket With Insurance

With coverage, most patients pay their standard specialist copays or coinsurance. Total out-of-pocket after meeting your deductible is typically $500–$3,000 depending on your plan.

💳

Without Insurance

A full 36-session course costs approximately $7,000–$12,000 out of pocket. HSA and FSA funds are eligible. We do not accept insurance assignment but provide documentation for reimbursement claims.

Insurance note: Coverage varies by plan and individual circumstances. We verify your specific benefits before any treatment begins. The above reflects typical coverage — not a guarantee for your plan.
