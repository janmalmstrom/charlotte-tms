## 3. Core Problem We Solve

Roughly 30% of people with major depressive disorder do not respond adequately to antidepressants. TMS offers a non-invasive, FDA-cleared alternative that works through direct neural stimulation — no medication, no systemic side effects, no anesthesia. Most insurance covers it after documented antidepressant failure.

## 4. Positioning

**One sentence:** Charlotte's FDA-cleared TMS clinic — non-invasive depression treatment covered by most insurance.

**vs. antidepressants:** TMS stimulates underactive brain circuits directly (left DLPFC). No systemic drug side effects. Works when medications have not.

**vs. ketamine:** TMS is FDA-cleared (ketamine is off-label for depression). Insurance typically covers TMS; ketamine is cash-pay. TMS has no dissociative effect — patients drive themselves to and from every session.

**vs. ECT:** TMS is non-invasive. No anesthesia. No memory loss. Patients are awake and alert during every session.

**vs. doing nothing:** Treatment-resistant depression carries significant mortality risk. TMS produces a meaningful response in approximately 50–60% of treatment-resistant patients — a clinically significant improvement over adding another antidepressant.

## 8. Tone of Voice

**Personality:** Board-certified psychiatrist. Direct, factual, warm without being soft. Explains the mechanism. Never oversells.

**For patients who have tried everything:** Direct and practical. They've been through antidepressant after antidepressant. Don't make them search for the answer.

**On insurance:** Be specific and honest. Most major insurers cover TMS after 2+ antidepressant failures with a treatment-resistant depression diagnosis. Coverage is not guaranteed — patients need to verify their specific plan.

**Good:** "TMS has been FDA-cleared for treatment-resistant depression since 2008. Most major insurers — BCBS, Cigna, Aetna, UnitedHealthcare — cover it after two documented antidepressant failures. Roughly 58% of treatment-resistant patients show a meaningful response."

## 9. Content Rules

- Lead with the clinical fact, not the emotional pitch.
- FDA-cleared, not FDA-approved — TMS uses 510(k) clearance, not the NDA approval process. The distinction matters medically.
- Always cite named research: NIMH, APA practice guidelines, O'Reardon et al. 2007 multisite trial, George et al. 2010, specific NeuroStar/Brainsway trial data.
- Specific numbers required: "58% response rate" not "many patients improve." "36 sessions" not "multiple sessions."
- Insurance facts: name the major insurers (BCBS NC, Cigna, Aetna, UHC), state the standard criteria (2+ antidepressant failures, DSM-5 MDD diagnosis).
- Name real Charlotte/Mecklenburg context: Mecklenburg County, Atrium Health, Novant Health, UNCC, Bank of America, Truist, the I-77/I-85 corridor.
- Never overstate: ~40% of patients do not respond. Be honest about that.
- One CTA per article: call (704) 555-0100 or request consultation form.
- Medical Disclaimer on all articles.
