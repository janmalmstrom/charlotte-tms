What is the Stanford SAINT protocol and how effective is it?

Quick Answer: The Stanford SAINT protocol is an accelerated, high-dose TMS schedule that delivered remission in ~79% of severely depressed patients in Cole et al. 2022, compared to ~58% response rates in standard TMS trials.

Key Takeaways

  • SAINT (Stanford Accelerated Intelligent Neuromodulation Therapy) delivers 10 TMS sessions per day for 5 consecutive days — 50 sessions total — compared to the standard 36-session, 6–8 week schedule.
  • In the Cole et al. 2022 randomized controlled trial published in the American Journal of Psychiatry, 78.6% of participants receiving SAINT met remission criteria, versus 13.3% receiving sham treatment.
  • Each SAINT session uses intermittent theta burst stimulation (iTBS), the same protocol validated as non-inferior to standard TMS in Blumberger et al. 2018 (The Lancet).
  • SAINT is currently available only at select specialized centers; most Charlotte-area patients pursuing accelerated TMS will follow a modified iTBS schedule rather than the full Stanford 5-day protocol.
  • Standard TMS — 36 sessions over 6–8 weeks — remains the insurance-covered benchmark at most clinics; SAINT-based protocols are generally self-pay and range from $3,000–$6,000 for the full course.

What Is the Stanford SAINT Protocol?

SAINT stands for Stanford Accelerated Intelligent Neuromodulation Therapy. It is a high-dose accelerated TMS protocol developed at Stanford University's Brain Stimulation Laboratory. Standard TMS delivers one session per day over 6–8 weeks (typically 36 sessions), whereas SAINT compresses treatment into 10 sessions per day across 5 consecutive days — 50 sessions total. Each session lasts approximately 10 minutes using intermittent theta burst stimulation (iTBS), with the full daily treatment block taking roughly 90–100 minutes including inter-session intervals.

SAINT incorporates functional MRI (fMRI) targeting to individualize the precise subregion of the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC) stimulated — the neural circuit implicated in treatment-resistant major depressive disorder (MDD). This personalized targeting distinguishes SAINT from off-the-shelf accelerated TMS schedules that use iTBS without fMRI guidance.

What Does the Research Actually Show?

The most rigorous evidence comes from Cole et al. 2022, published in the American Journal of Psychiatry, a randomized, double-blind, sham-controlled trial. Of 29 participants receiving active SAINT, 78.6% met remission criteria on the Montgomery-Åsberg Depression Rating Scale (MADRS) at the primary endpoint. In the sham group, 13.3% met remission criteria. Participants had failed an average of 2.5 antidepressant trials — a population representative of many patients seeking TMS in Charlotte.

An earlier open-label pilot (Cole et al. 2020, PNAS) reported similarly high remission rates and found benefits appeared within days of completing the protocol — notably faster than the 4–6 week response window typical of standard TMS.

The iTBS waveform underlying SAINT sessions was validated as non-inferior to conventional 10 Hz TMS in the Blumberger et al. 2018 THREE-D trial in The Lancet, which enrolled 414 patients across multiple sites. That study reported iTBS response rates comparable to standard TMS at approximately 49% vs. 47% — relevant because SAINT builds its treatment density on the same waveform.

💡 Clinical note: The 78.6% remission figure from Cole et al. 2022 comes from a relatively small trial (n=29 active treatment). Larger multi-site replication studies are ongoing. Clinicians appropriately present this data with both enthusiasm for the signal and appropriate caution regarding generalizability.

How Does SAINT Differ from Standard TMS and Accelerated iTBS?

Standard TMS — FDA-cleared for treatment-resistant depression since 2008 — delivers 3,000 pulses per session, once daily, five days per week, for approximately 7 weeks. SAINT delivers 1,800 iTBS pulses per session, 10 sessions per day, for 5 days, totaling 90,000 pulses across the full course. The compressed timeline is the primary practical distinction.

The fMRI targeting component is the second major distinction. Most accelerated TMS programs across North Carolina target the left DLPFC using standardized neuronavigation or the "F3 method" — a validated landmark-based approach. Full SAINT, as performed at Stanford, uses individualized resting-state fMRI to identify each patient's optimal stimulation site within the DLPFC. This adds both precision and logistical complexity that most outpatient TMS centers in Charlotte are not equipped to replicate.

SAINT vs. Standard TMS: Key Differences at a Glance

  • Sessions: SAINT = 50 sessions over 5 days; Standard = 36 sessions over ~7 weeks
  • Daily time commitment: SAINT = ~90–100 min/day for 5 days; Standard = ~20–40 min/day for 30+ days
  • Waveform: Both use iTBS or standard 10 Hz rTMS; SAINT specifies iTBS
  • Targeting: SAINT uses fMRI-guided personalized targeting; standard TMS uses neuronavigation or F3 landmark method
  • Insurance coverage: Standard TMS = typically covered by BCBS NC, Cigna, Aetna, UHC, Medicare after 2+ antidepressant failures; SAINT = generally self-pay
  • Remission data: SAINT ~79% (Cole et al. 2022, n=29 active); Standard TMS ~37% remission, ~58% response (real-world data)

Is SAINT Available in Charlotte, and Who Qualifies?

Full Stanford SAINT — with real-time fMRI targeting — is not widely available outside academic medical centers. In North Carolina, programs with fMRI-guided capabilities remain affiliated with academic health systems. Charlotte's healthcare systems, including Atrium Health and Novant Health, maintain strong psychiatry programs, but fMRI-guided SAINT as performed at Stanford is not a standard offering at most outpatient TMS clinics in Mecklenburg County as of 2024.

What is accessible in Charlotte is accelerated iTBS — a modified, high-density protocol sharing SAINT's compressed timeline and waveform without the individualized fMRI component. For patients in demanding roles who cannot take 7 weeks away from full productivity, an accelerated iTBS schedule offers a meaningful practical advantage over the standard 6–8 week course.

Licensed Charlotte TMS providers can clarify which accelerated protocols they offer and whether your specific situation — including prior antidepressant failures and current diagnosis — makes you a candidate. Request a consultation to get a direct answer on local protocol availability.

What Does SAINT-Based TMS Cost, and Does Insurance Cover It?

Standard TMS — 36 sessions, once daily — is covered by most major insurers after two documented antidepressant failures with a DSM-5 MDD diagnosis. BCBS NC, Cigna, Aetna, UnitedHealthcare, and Medicare all have established coverage policies for standard TMS. Employees of self-insured large employers should verify their specific plan documents, as self-insured plans set their own medical necessity criteria.

SAINT-based protocols typically fall outside current insurance coverage criteria because they deviate from the FDA-cleared standard course and the fMRI component adds cost. Self-pay costs for a full accelerated protocol range from approximately $3,000 to $6,000 depending on the number of sessions, equipment, and clinic pricing. Modified accelerated iTBS courses without fMRI guidance generally cost less than full SAINT. Your provider will clarify exact pricing during consultation.

⚠️ Important: SAINT's 78.6% remission figure comes from a single-site trial of 29 active-treatment participants. While the result is clinically significant and the methodology is rigorous, it should not be interpreted as a guaranteed outcome. Approximately 21% of trial participants did not achieve remission. Larger multi-site trials are underway. A board-certified psychiatrist should review your specific history before drawing conclusions about your likely response.
Bottom Line

The Stanford SAINT protocol represents a promising development in TMS research — a compressed, high-dose iTBS schedule with remission rates meaningfully higher than what standard TMS trials have reported. The Cole et al. 2022 RCT shows 78.6% remission in treatment-resistant patients over 5 days. Full SAINT with fMRI targeting is not yet widely available in the Charlotte area, but modified accelerated iTBS schedules are offered by select local providers and may suit patients who need faster results or have scheduling constraints. Standard TMS — 36 sessions, covered by most major insurers including BCBS NC, Cigna, Aetna, and UHC — remains the benchmark for insurance coverage and has solid evidence going back to O'Reardon et al. 2007. Approximately 42% of treatment-resistant patients do not respond adequately to standard TMS; SAINT's preliminary data suggests this proportion may improve, though larger trials will confirm the magnitude.
